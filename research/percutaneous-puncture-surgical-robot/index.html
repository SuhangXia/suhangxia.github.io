Method · Calibration, navigation, metrology

System Calibration & Accuracy Verification

The same geometric account connects manufactured fixtures, CT landmarks, tracked instruments, and robot motion. An independent laser-tracker measurement then checks where the tool actually arrived.

01 / Fixture & image registration

Six sockets. Six divots.
Two distinct jobs.

The machined fixture has six removable pillars with hemispherical sockets A–F, and six sampling divots a–f. Coordinate measuring machine (CMM) measurements establish the assembled socket-centre and divot coordinates as geometric ground truth.

Metal spheres seated on the pillars make their centres recoverable in CT. The six divots are selected directly in CT and sampled with a pivot-calibrated NDI probe for landmark registration. Twenty additional surface samples refine the image-to-fixture alignment through ICP.

02 / Tool geometry & robot calibration

A calibrated origin.
A selected tool axis.

The cross-shaped metal tool carries three hemispherical sockets and three adjacent conical divots. CMM measurements establish the socket centres and conical apices. Sampling those three apices with the calibrated NDI probe registers the known tool geometry to the tool’s tracked marker.

Robot hand–eye calibration connects optical tracking to robot motion. One selected tool sphere centre becomes the TCP origin; another defines the axis. The target is an origin and a direction—not a requirement that both tool sphere centres coincide with both fixture targets, and not a complete six-degree-of-freedom orientation defined by two points alone.

03 / Independent measurement

Remove the pillars.
Keep the reference.

Before removing any pillars, a Leica spherically mounted retroreflector (SMR) is placed into each pillar socket in turn to measure and store its centre position. The fixture base remains fixed while the pillars are unscrewed, leaving the target space clear for the robot-mounted tool.

The robot moves the selected TCP origin to the planned target and aligns the tool axis with the planned line. Once it arrives, an SMR seated in the tool sockets measures the actual tool sphere centres. Reference and actual coordinates are compared in the same valid Leica measurement frame, independently of the NDI navigation estimate.

Instrument principle: Leica / Hexagon laser-tracker metrology ↗

Three errors. One measurement frame.

Let pA and pB be the pre-removal Leica reference centres, and q0 and q1 the measured tool centres after arrival. Unit vectors u and v follow the corresponding reference and tool axes, with consistent origin-to-second-point ordering.

01 / Position · mm

Point-to-point

dpp = ‖q0 − pA‖

Euclidean distance from the actual TCP origin to the target origin.

02 / Offset · mm

Point-to-line

dpl = ‖(q0 − pA) × u‖

Perpendicular distance from the actual TCP origin to the planned reference line.

03 / Direction · degrees

Axis angular error

θ = cos−1(u · v)

Angle between the reference and measured tool axes, expressed in degrees.

These definitions explain the inspection algorithm. The retained slide requirements below are not a table of measured Leica results; no unprovided inspection values are inferred.

Preclinical Animal Study

Designed and led
beyond the bench.

I designed and led the animal experiments as project leader, taking the team to collaborate with Silver Snake Clinical Center in Guangzhou. The reported comparison recorded lower mean error and higher first-attempt puncture success for robot assistance than for conventional CT guidance.
